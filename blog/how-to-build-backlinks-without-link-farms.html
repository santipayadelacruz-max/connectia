Backlinks
still matter.
Link farms still don't work.

The legitimate, if slower, methods for building backlinks that actually hold up rather than risking a penalty down the line.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz• October 13, 2026• 8 min read

Backlinks remain one of the more durable authority signals in search, and that hasn't changed even as the field has evolved considerably, even as the methods for earning them legitimately have had to adapt considerably alongside it. What has genuinely changed is how effectively Google now detects manipulative link schemes, which makes the shortcuts that used to work, link farms, paid link networks, excessive reciprocal linking, considerably riskier now than they were years ago, and considerably more likely to trigger a manual action that costs far more than the shortcut ever saved.

Digital PR as the highest-leverage legitimate method

Original research, genuinely newsworthy data, or a distinctive perspective on an industry trend, pitched carefully to the right journalists and publications, earns links the way they were originally meant to be earned, as a natural citation of something genuinely worth referencing in its own right. This is more labour-intensive than buying links, but the links it produces come from higher-authority sources and carry none of the penalty risk that manipulative tactics do.

Genuinely useful free tools and resources

A calculator, template, checklist or tool that solves a real, specific problem tends to earn organic links from people who found it useful and want to reference it, without any outreach required at all in many cases. This requires more upfront investment to build than a blog post, but it can generate backlinks passively for years afterward, unlike content that has to be actively pitched every time.

Contributing genuine expertise to other publications

Guest content for genuinely relevant, reputable publications, real expertise contributed rather than thin content submitted purely for the link, builds authority and earns a legitimate link back in the process. The distinction that matters here is quality and relevance over volume, a handful of genuinely well-placed contributions outperform dozens of low-quality guest posts on unrelated or low-authority sites.

Building relationships within an industry, not just chasing links directly

Genuine relationships with journalists, other businesses and industry commentators produce link opportunities organically over time in a way that transactional, one-off outreach rarely does. This is slower and less directly measurable than a single outreach campaign, but it tends to compound, since each genuine relationship can produce multiple link opportunities over months or years rather than a single transaction.

What to actively avoid

Paid link networks, private blog networks, excessive link exchanges, and any scheme explicitly designed to manipulate rankings rather than earn genuine editorial citation all carry real, growing risk of a manual action that can cost far more in recovery time than the shortcut ever saved in effort. If a link-building tactic requires paying specifically for the link itself, rather than for genuine promotion or genuine content creation, it's worth treating that as a clear warning sign rather than a shortcut worth ever taking, since the underlying SEO foundation a manual action damages takes far longer to rebuild than any link scheme ever saved.

The long game this approach requires

None of the legitimate methods above produce backlinks overnight, and that's precisely the trade-off for avoiding the penalty risk that comes with faster, manipulative alternatives. Businesses that commit to this slower, legitimate path consistently end up with a link profile that holds up under scrutiny rather than one that carries a permanent risk of collapsing during the next algorithm update focused on link quality.
