Landing page checklist
for paid ads:
clicks into quotes.

You pay for every click, so the page that receives it has to convince fast. These are the elements that matter most.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 21, 2026•8 min read

One page, one goal

A landing page is not your website. It has a single goal, such as requesting a quote or calling, with no distracting menus. Every element should push toward that action.

If you have several goals, build several pages.

Message match

Visitors expect to see what the ad promised. Repeat the message in the headline in the same words. If the ad says 'bathroom renovation in [city]', so should the page.

This improves conversion and ad quality.

Proof and trust

Real reviews, client logos, case studies or certifications reduce doubt. Place them near the form.

Avoid invented testimonials: they show and damage trust.

Short, clear form

Ask for the minimum: name, contact and one question. Each extra field lowers conversions. Explain what happens next and when you will reply.

On mobile, a visible call button adds a lot.

Speed and mobile

A slow page wastes clicks you already paid for. Optimise images, remove unneeded scripts and test on mobile. Most paid traffic arrives on phones.

Speed also affects ad quality.

Tracking

Set up conversion tracking from day one: forms, calls and messaging clicks. Without it you cannot optimise.

Review which ads and keywords bring enquiries.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Sending traffic to the home page.
  • Long forms.
  • Message different from the ad.
  • Not tracking calls.
  • Slow mobile page.

Mistake number one

Sending ad traffic to the homepage. The homepage talks about everything; the ad promised one thing. If people do not see what was promised in five seconds, they leave.

One page, one goal, one button. Everything else is noise.
