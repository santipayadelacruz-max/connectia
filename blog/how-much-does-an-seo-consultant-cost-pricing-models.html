How much does
an SEO consultant cost?
Pricing models explained.

Nobody can quote you honestly without seeing your site and your market. But the three ways SEO is priced are easy to understand, and each suits a different situation.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 20, 2026•8 min read

Why prices vary so much

SEO is not a boxed product. Price depends on your industry, competition, site size, starting point and goals. A local business in a low-competition niche needs far less work than an online store in a crowded market.

That is why understanding pricing models is more useful than hunting for an average figure.

Model 1: fixed-price project

You agree a scope and a price for an audit, a migration or an initial optimisation. It suits well-defined problems: you know what you get, when and for how much.

The limitation is that it does not cover ongoing work. Many clients start with a project and later move to a monthly agreement.

Model 2: monthly retainer

A monthly fee covers a defined set of tasks such as content, optimisation, links and reporting. It is the usual model when SEO is a continuous strategy.

It needs clarity on what is done each month and how it is measured. A retainer without deliverables is a warning sign.

Model 3: hourly or advisory

Useful for one-off questions, training or reviews. You pay for time and keep flexibility. The risk is unpredictability if the scope is not capped.

It works well when you need support for your in-house team rather than a full service.

What every proposal should include

Detailed scope, deliverables, timelines, reporting, what the consultant needs from you and how success is measured. If a proposal talks only about price, ask for detail.

Also ask who owns accounts, content and tools when the engagement ends.

How to choose the right model

If you do not know the state of your site, start with an audit. If you know what to fix and want it done, choose a project. If you are competing over the long term, choose a retainer.

A good consultant recommends the model that fits you, even when it is not the most expensive.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Comparing prices without comparing scope.
  • Signing a retainer with no deliverables.
  • Measuring rankings but not enquiries.
  • Accepting long lock-ins without milestones.
  • Expecting instant results.

What I would choose, and when

For a defined problem such as an audit or a migration, a fixed project. To keep results growing, a monthly agreement with clear goals. Hourly is fine for questions.

I distrust long contracts with no exit. I would rather you stay because it works than because you cannot leave.
