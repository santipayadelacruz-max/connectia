Ranking for a keyword
means nothing
if it doesn't convert.

A practical framework for choosing SEO keywords based on actual buying intent, not just how many people search for them.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz• August 4, 2026• 7 min read

A keyword with ten thousand monthly searches and near-zero commercial intent can bring a lot of traffic and almost no business result. A keyword with two hundred searches and clear buying intent behind it often converts at a rate the bigger term never will, which makes it a far better use of limited content resources despite looking unimpressive on a volume report. Search volume is the easiest number to find and the least reliable one to lead a keyword strategy with, which is exactly why it ends up driving so many strategies anyway, purely out of convenience.

Reading intent behind a keyword before targeting it

Informational searches, what is, how does, why does, generally sit early in someone's research and rarely convert directly, though they're valuable for building topical authority and brand awareness. Commercial and transactional searches, best, vs, near me, pricing, buy, sit much closer to a decision and typically convert at a meaningfully higher rate, even at a fraction of the search volume.

Checking what's actually ranking, not just the keyword's stats

The search results themselves are the most reliable signal of intent, more reliable than any keyword tool's intent label. If the top results for a term are all blog posts and general guides, the underlying intent is informational, regardless of what a tool's classification says. If the top results are product or service pages, that's a much stronger commercial signal worth prioritising.

Why competitive difficulty matters less than it seems for smaller businesses

A high-difficulty score often reflects competition from large, established sites that a smaller or newer business genuinely can't outrank in the near term, regardless of content quality. Specific, longer-tail variations of a core commercial term, with lower search volume but also lower competition, frequently deliver a better realistic ROI than chasing a competitive head term that's unlikely to move for months or years.

Matching keywords to actual business goals, not just traffic targets

A keyword strategy built purely around maximising total traffic often optimises for the wrong outcome entirely, since a bigger traffic number on its own says nothing about whether the right people are actually arriving. Carefully mapping keywords against where they sit in an actual buyer's journey, and against which specific pages the business wants more of the right kind of traffic to reach, keeps the strategy anchored to revenue rather than to a vanity traffic number that looks good in a report but doesn't move the business forward at all.

A practical starting checklist

  • Search the term yourself first and genuinely assess what kind of pages currently rank for it
  • Prioritise terms with clear commercial or transactional intent over pure informational volume, at least for pages meant to drive revenue directly
  • Weigh realistic achievable difficulty against a business's actual current authority, not just against the raw competition score a tool assigns to the term
  • Map every target keyword to a specific business outcome, not just to a traffic number on a dashboard

A note on keyword tools versus judgement

Keyword tools are useful for surfacing options and rough volume, but the intent classification they assign is often generic and worth treating as a starting hypothesis rather than a final answer. Reading the actual search results for a shortlist of candidate terms, a five-minute manual step, catches nuance that no automated tool reliably captures, and it's a habit worth building into any keyword research process rather than skipping in favour of trusting the tool's label outright, especially for terms that will anchor an important page or receive meaningful ad spend pointed at them later.
