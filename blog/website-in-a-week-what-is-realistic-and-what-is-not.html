A website in a week:
what is realistic
and what is not.

Yes, a good website in a week is possible. A shop with 500 products is not. Here is where the line sits.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 24, 2026•8 min read

What fits in a week

With a defined scope, a week allows a site of up to about ten pages: home, services, about, results, FAQ and contact, with mobile-first design, SEO basics, forms and tracking.

The key is preparation. If content arrives on day one and decisions are quick, the calendar holds.

How the week is split

Day one: kickoff call, sitemap and content collection. Days two and three: copy and design of key pages as a live preview. Days four and five: full build with forms, tracking and on-page SEO. Day six: your review. Day seven: launch, Search Console and handover.

Phases overlap so no time is lost and your review is built in.

What does not fit

Long copy from scratch, professional photo or video shoots, complex integrations and a large shop do not fit a week. Nor does a full rebrand.

They can be planned as a second phase with their own timeline and budget so the launch is not compromised.

Who it suits

It suits you if you need to be online soon, know what you offer, can supply content on time and accept a focused scope.

It does not suit you if content will take days, there is a long approval chain or you want to decide structure as you go.

What to prepare

Logo and colours, copy or key ideas for services, your own photos or permission to use stock images, contact details and access to your domain.

If you lack copy, it can be drafted from a few questions, but allow time to review it.

How to avoid surprises

Ask for scope in writing: pages, features, number of reviews and exclusions. Agree who decides and when review happens. Confirm who owns the domain and content.

A short timeline with no defined scope is an empty promise.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Asking for a week with no content.
  • Adding pages mid-project.
  • Reviewing in many small rounds.
  • Skipping mobile testing.
  • Forgetting SEO basics.

Where I draw the line

A site of four to six pages with careful design, clear copy, a form, your Google profile connected and basic SEO fits. A large shop, unusual integrations or migrating a huge site do not.

And one condition: content arrives quickly. If I have it on day one, you have a site by day seven; if it takes a week to arrive, the deadline is not in my hands.
