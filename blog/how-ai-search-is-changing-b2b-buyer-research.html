AI search is quietly changing how B2B buyers shortlist vendors, well before a sales conversation ever happens.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz• October 27, 2026• 7 min read

A meaningful share of B2B buyers, across a growing range of industries, now start vendor research with an AI tool rather than a traditional Google search, asking a conversational question and getting back a synthesised answer, sometimes with a shortlist of specific vendors already suggested, before a search results page ever enters the picture. This shifts real influence earlier in the buying process than most marketing strategies currently account for.

Shortlists are forming before a website ever gets visited

A buyer asking an AI tool to compare options in a category may arrive at a shortlist of two or three vendors before visiting a single company website directly, which is a genuinely different starting point than the funnel most marketing plans still assume. Being excluded from that early, AI-generated shortlist means a business may never get the chance to make its case at all, regardless of how strong its website and sales process actually are once someone finally reaches them.

The questions being asked are getting more specific

Rather than a broad "best CRM software" search, buyers increasingly ask specific, contextual questions, "best CRM for a 20-person sales team that already uses HubSpot marketing", that an AI tool can synthesise an answer to directly. Content built around these specific, contextual scenarios has a meaningfully better chance of being the source an AI model draws from for that particular kind of nuanced query.

Trust signals matter earlier in the funnel than before

If an AI tool is effectively doing initial vetting before a human buyer engages directly, the authority and trust signals that influence whether a business gets included in that vetting, genuine case studies, transparent pricing information, verifiable client outcomes, matter earlier in the funnel than they used to, when they were more commonly reserved for later-stage sales conversations.

Sales teams increasingly need to know what the AI told the buyer

A buyer who's already had an AI-generated summary of a category before a first sales call arrives with different assumptions and expectations than one starting from a blank slate. Sales teams that ask directly what research the buyer has already done, including any AI tools consulted, can address specific claims, corrections or comparisons the AI surfaced rather than repeating a generic pitch the buyer has effectively already half-formed an opinion on.

What this means for content and marketing strategy

Content built to directly answer specific, realistic buyer questions, structured clearly enough for an AI model to extract and cite confidently, matters more than ever, since it's competing not just for a Google ranking but for inclusion in an AI-generated shortlist that may be the only exposure a buyer gets before narrowing their options. This is precisely the shift GEO strategy is built to address directly, treating AI visibility as a genuine part of the buyer journey rather than a separate, secondary concern from traditional marketing.

Preparing for a future where this only grows

Businesses that wait until AI-assisted buyer research is obviously dominant before adapting will be responding to a trend already fully in motion rather than getting ahead of it. Starting now, even with modest steps, content built around specific buyer questions and sales conversations that ask directly what research has already happened, builds a meaningful head start while competitors are still treating this as a future concern rather than a present one.
