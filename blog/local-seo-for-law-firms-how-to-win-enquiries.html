Local SEO for law firms:
how to win
more enquiries.

People looking for a lawyer are usually under pressure and cautious. The firm that explains clearly and is easy to find gets the call.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 14, 2026•8 min read

How clients search for a lawyer

Clients search by problem and place: 'divorce lawyer in [city]', 'commercial solicitor near me', 'employment law firm [city]'. Each phrase maps to a practice area, so your site needs a page for each one.

They also compare firms. Trust signals decide who gets the call.

One page per practice area

Write a page per practice area with original content: who it is for, how the process works, typical timelines, what to prepare and a simple way to ask for a consultation. Avoid thin or duplicated pages with only the city changed.

Explain without jargon. Clear writing converts better than impressive wording.

Google Business Profile

For local intent, the profile is often the first thing a client sees. Choose the right categories, list services, add photos of the office and team, and keep opening hours accurate. Link to the relevant practice pages.

Respond to every review in a professional way, mindful of confidentiality.

Credentials and trust

Partner profiles, qualifications, memberships, published articles and case outcomes where permitted all build credibility. Place them near contact forms and on practice pages.

Follow your regulator's rules on advertising and testimonials.

Enquiry paths

Offer a short form, a visible phone number and a clear next step. Explain what happens after an enquiry and how fast you reply. Make mobile contact effortless.

Track calls and forms so you know which pages produce clients.

Measurement

Measure enquiries, calls from the profile and consultations booked, not only visits. Review monthly which practice areas and pages perform best.

Use the results to decide where to invest next.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Thin practice pages.
  • Ignoring the profile.
  • No credentials shown.
  • Ignoring advertising rules.
  • Measuring only rankings.

One page per practice area

Someone facing a divorce does not want to read about commercial law. Each area needs its own page: the problem, the process, rough timescales and how to get in touch.

Show who they will talk to, and avoid promising outcomes. It is both ethics and regulation.
