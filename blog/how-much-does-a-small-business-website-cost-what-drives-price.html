How much does
a small business website
cost?

A small business site can cost a few hundred pounds or a few thousand, and both can be right. What changes is what you actually receive.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 22, 2026•8 min read

Why there is no single price

Two sites that look similar can cost very different amounts because you pay for more than what you see: pages, design work, copywriting, technical set-up, SEO and professional time.

A price without a scope means nothing. Before comparing figures, compare what each proposal includes.

The factors that matter most

These elements move the final price most:

  • Number of pages and the complexity of each.
  • Custom design versus an adapted template.
  • Who writes the copy and supplies images.
  • Extra features: bookings, shop, client area, languages.
  • SEO included from day one or later.
  • Timeline: urgent projects need reserved time.

What a serious quote includes

A clear quote details scope page by page, rounds of changes, timeline, what the designer needs from you and what is excluded. It also states who owns the domain, hosting and content at the end.

If a proposal just says 'business website' and a number, ask for detail. Ambiguity tends to become extras later.

Signs a quote is too cheap

A price far below the rest can mean an untouched template, generic copy, no SEO or lock-in to a provider you cannot leave. Sometimes it is a good deal, but ask what is being cut.

Ask for examples, test their speed on mobile and check whether you can edit content yourself.

Signs a quote is inflated

A high price does not guarantee quality. Be wary if the work behind it is not explained, if it includes services you do not need or if scope cannot be adjusted to your budget.

A good proposal allows a focused first version and a second phase to expand using real data.

How to get comparable quotes

Write a short brief and send it to everyone: what you do, who you serve, pages you imagine, features, example sites you like and a deadline. Proposals then become comparable.

Always ask for scope, timeline and price in writing. A fixed price is better: you know what you pay and what you get.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Choosing on price alone.
  • No written scope.
  • Not knowing who owns the domain.
  • Forgetting SEO and rebuilding later.
  • Not reserving time to supply content.

How I would compare quotes

Compare deliverables, not prices: how many pages, who writes the copy, whether basic SEO is included, and what happens after launch. Two quotes of 1,500 can be completely different things.

And ask who owns everything. Domain, hosting and content should be in your name, or you are renting your own website.
