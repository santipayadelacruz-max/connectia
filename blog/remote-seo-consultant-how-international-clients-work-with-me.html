Hiring a remote
SEO consultant:
how it works.

Most SEO work does not need anyone in your office. Here is how a remote engagement runs when you and I are in different countries.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 27, 2026•7 min read

Why SEO works well remotely

SEO is done in analytics tools, content systems and shared documents. None of it requires a physical presence. What matters is clarity, response speed and a process both sides understand.

Remote work also removes travel and local overheads, which keeps costs lower than a locally based agency of similar quality.

How an engagement runs

It starts with a free thirty-minute video call about goals and market. Then comes an audit and plan, a written proposal with scope, timeline and fixed price, and execution in sprints with regular reporting.

Everything is documented in shared files so your team always knows what is happening.

Time zones and communication

Working with clients from Europe to North America is practical with a few agreed windows for calls and asynchronous updates in between. Most questions are answered in writing within a business day.

Agree a primary channel and a regular call, and the time difference stops being a problem.

Languages and local markets

I work in English and Spanish. For other languages, I advise on structure, keywords and hreflang, and work with your native writers or translators so the content reads naturally.

Local search behaviour differs by country, so research is done for your market, not copied from another.

Privacy and compliance

Tracking and forms are set up with local privacy rules in mind, such as GDPR, the UK GDPR, CCPA or Swiss nDSG: consent, minimal data and clear policies. I am not a lawyer, so final wording should be confirmed with your legal adviser.

Access to your accounts is limited to what is needed and can be revoked at any time.

Reporting you can use

Reports focus on enquiries, calls and revenue-linked metrics rather than vanity figures. They are written for decision makers, not only for SEOs.

You see what was done, what moved and what comes next.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Assuming remote means slow.
  • No agreed channel or rhythm.
  • Not clarifying who owns accounts.
  • Ignoring local privacy rules.
  • Measuring traffic instead of enquiries.

What makes it work

A clear plan, written updates, and a short call when something needs a decision. You do not need daily meetings; you need to know what is happening and what comes next.

Time zones matter less than people think. Agree a weekly rhythm and stick to it.

Working across borders

I work in English and Spanish with companies in several countries. If you would like to talk about yours, ask me for a quote.
