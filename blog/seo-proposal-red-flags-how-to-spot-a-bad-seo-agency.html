red flags:
spot a bad agency.

Most bad SEO engagements show the same warning signs in the proposal. Spot them before you sign and you will save months and money.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 25, 2026•8 min read

Why red flags matter

SEO is hard to evaluate in advance, so the proposal is your main evidence. A vague or overpromising proposal usually leads to a vague or disappointing engagement.

The good news is that most warning signs are visible on paper.

Guaranteed rankings

No honest consultant can guarantee a position on Google, because results depend on competition and on factors nobody controls. Promises like 'first page in 30 days' are a sign of either ignorance or sales pressure.

A good proposal commits to a plan, process and reporting, not to a ranking.

Vague deliverables

Phrases like 'on-page optimisation' or 'link building' with no detail hide what you will actually receive. Ask: how many pages, what changes, what content, what reporting?

If the answer is evasive, assume the work will be too.

Secret methods and bulk links

Be wary of 'proprietary techniques' you cannot be told about, or promises of large numbers of links. Low-quality links can harm a site.

Legitimate SEO can be explained clearly in plain language.

Lock-in and ownership

Long contracts with no milestones, no access to your own analytics or accounts, and unclear ownership of content are all red flags. You should be able to leave with your data and your content.

Ask what happens to pages and accounts if you stop.

Reporting that hides the truth

Reports full of impressions and keyword counts but no calls, form submissions or sales can look impressive while meaning little. Ask for reporting tied to business outcomes.

A good report is understandable by someone who is not an SEO.

Common mistakes when evaluating

The most frequent errors:

  • Choosing the biggest promise.
  • Not asking who does the work.
  • Skipping references.
  • Ignoring contract terms.
  • Not checking how success is measured.

What a good proposal looks like

  • Goal tied to enquiries or sales.
  • Clear scope and deliverables.
  • Honest timeline.
  • Reporting you can understand.
  • Fair exit terms.
  • You own accounts and content.

The one I trust least

A guaranteed ranking. Nobody controls Google, so anyone promising position one is either lying or targeting terms that are too easy to matter.

Second: no named person. If you do not know who will touch your site, ask, and expect a straight answer.

What I put in writing

Scope, timeline, price, reporting and ownership, all in plain words. Want to see an example for your case? ask me for a quote.
