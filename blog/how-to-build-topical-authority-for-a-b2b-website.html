Topical authority
isn't about
how much you publish.

A practical breakdown of what topical authority actually is, and how to build it deliberately instead of by accident.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz• June 2, 2026• 7 min read

Topical authority is the degree to which search engines, and increasingly AI models, treat a site as a genuine, trustworthy source on a specific subject rather than one page that happens to mention it. Two sites can each have a single article ranking for the same keyword, and one will hold that position far more durably than the other, because the surrounding site demonstrates depth on the topic and the other doesn't. That durability gap is exactly what makes topical authority worth building deliberately rather than hoping it accumulates as a side effect of publishing regularly.

Why depth on a narrow topic beats breadth across many

A B2B site covering fifteen loosely related topics shallowly rarely outcompetes one covering three topics thoroughly, because the second signals genuine expertise in a way the first can't. This runs against the intuition that more content means more chances to rank, but coverage that's obviously scattered across unrelated subjects tends to read as generic rather than authoritative, to both a search engine and a human reader.

Mapping the actual topic before writing anything

Building topical authority starts with mapping a subject properly: the core topic, the subtopics a genuine expert would naturally cover, and the specific questions a buyer at each stage of their research would ask. This map, built before any content gets written, prevents the common pattern of publishing whatever keyword idea comes up next without a coherent structure connecting the pieces.

Internal linking is what turns individual pages into a cluster

A set of well-written but unlinked pages doesn't build topical authority nearly as effectively as the same pages properly interlinked, since the links themselves are part of the signal that tells a search engine these pages belong to a coherent body of work. A central pillar page covering the topic broadly, linking out to and receiving links from the more specific supporting pages, is the structure that tends to work best in practice.

What actually demonstrates expertise, beyond just covering the topic

Original data, case studies from real client work, specific numbers rather than general claims, and content written by someone with a demonstrable, named background in the subject all signal genuine expertise in a way generic overview content doesn't. This is also where SEO content strategy and E-E-A-T overlap directly, since the same elements that build topical authority for search also build the trust signals that matter for AI visibility.

A realistic pace for B2B specifically

B2B topics are often narrower and more specialised than consumer ones, which means there's a genuine limit to how much content a topic can support before it starts becoming repetitive. Publishing two or three genuinely thorough pieces a month, each covering real ground, tends to build authority faster than publishing weekly at the cost of depth, particularly for a niche B2B subject where the total addressable content need is smaller than it looks. Once the core topic map is genuinely covered, the marginal value of yet another piece on the same ground drops sharply, which is worth recognising rather than padding a calendar for its own sake.
