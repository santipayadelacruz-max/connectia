How to hire
an SEO consultant:
12 questions to ask.

Hiring SEO means trusting someone with part of your lead generation. These twelve questions separate people who know the work from people who sell it.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 21, 2026•9 min read

Before you ask for proposals

Be clear on what you want: more calls, more quotes, more online sales. Without a goal, any proposal looks fine. With one, you can judge whether the plan makes sense.

Also set your horizon. SEO usually takes months to deliver lasting results, and your budget and patience should reflect that.

Questions about method

Ask how they would approach your case: what they analyse first, how they choose keywords, what they prioritise and why. A professional explains the process without hiding behind jargon.

Ask for a sample plan for a similar client, without confidential data. If everything is generic, be cautious.

Questions about results

Ask what results they expect and how they will measure them. No serious consultant promises first place or exact timelines. They can commit to a plan, clear reports and measuring enquiries, not only visits.

Ask for references or case studies with context: what was done, how long it took and what changed.

Questions about contract and reporting

Clarify minimum term, how to cancel, deliverables and who owns accounts and content. A transparent contract does not trap you.

Ask how often you will get reports and what they contain. A good report talks about enquiries, calls and priorities, not only charts.

Red flags

Be careful with these signs:

  • Guaranteed rankings or exact timelines.
  • Talk of secret techniques or mass links.
  • No explanation of what they will actually do.
  • Pressure to sign now.
  • No access to your own accounts.
  • A price with no scope.

Agency or independent consultant

An agency offers a team and structure; an independent consultant offers direct contact and often fewer layers. Neither is always better. It depends on project size and how involved you want to be.

Either way, make sure you know who will do the work, not only who sells it.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Choosing on price alone.
  • Not measuring enquiries.
  • Accepting reports you cannot understand.
  • Signing long lock-ins without review points.
  • Not asking who executes.

The three I would never skip

What exactly will you do in month one? How and when will you show me it worked? If I leave, do I keep my accounts, site and content?

If the answers are vague, or it is a proprietary method, walk away. Good SEO can be explained in plain words.
