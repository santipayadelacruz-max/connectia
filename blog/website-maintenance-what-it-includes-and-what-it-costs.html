Website maintenance:
what it includes
and costs.

A neglected website gets slow, insecure and out of date. It costs far less to prevent a problem than to fix a hacked site.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 20, 2026•7 min read

Why maintenance matters

A website is not a printed brochure. It needs security updates, backups, speed checks and small content changes. Without them, failures appear and cost visits and clients.

Reasonable maintenance fixes issues before customers notice.

What it usually includes

A basic plan covers:

  • Platform and plugin updates.
  • Backups.
  • Uptime monitoring.
  • Speed checks.
  • Small content changes.
  • Support for incidents.

What is usually excluded

Redesigns, new features, SEO campaigns and large copywriting are normally quoted separately. Clarify the boundary early to avoid disputes.

Ask how many hours of changes are included per month.

Options

You can buy a monthly plan with included hours, pay per incident or do it yourself with a guide. Each suits a different profile.

If the site is critical to the business, a plan with fast response usually pays off.

Comparing proposals

Compare scope, response time, number of changes, how backups work and what happens if something fails. Price only makes sense in that context.

Get it in writing.

When you can skip it

If your site is very simple and you are comfortable editing, you may skip a monthly plan and review it yourself regularly.

Even then keep backups and accounts in your name.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • No backups.
  • Months of pending updates.
  • No accounts in your name.
  • Buying without knowing the scope.
  • Waiting for something to break.

What I would include as a minimum

Automatic backups, updates, security and speed checks, and a small bank of hours for changes. Plus alerts if something breaks.

Be wary of maintenance plans that do not say what they do each month. If there is no report, there is no visible work.
