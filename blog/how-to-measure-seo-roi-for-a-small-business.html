How to measure
SEO ROI
for a small business.

Without measurement, SEO looks like a cost. A simple setup shows what it brings in and where to put the next pound.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 19, 2026•8 min read

What ROI means for SEO

It compares revenue attributable to SEO with what you invest. Unlike ads, SEO compounds: work done now keeps paying for months. Measure over several months, not weeks.

A single month rarely tells the story.

What to track, in order

Track these in sequence:

  • Impressions and clicks in Search Console.
  • Organic visits to key pages.
  • Enquiries: forms, calls, messages.
  • Opportunities and sales.
  • Revenue linked to organic.

Set up tracking first

Before measuring return, track form submissions, calls and contact clicks in your analytics. Without it SEO will appear to bring nothing even when it does.

Add a 'how did you hear about us' question to forms and calls.

Attributing sales

For online sales, organic revenue is measured directly. If you close by contact, record each client's source in a CRM or spreadsheet and match it to analytics.

Attribution is never perfect; a reasonable estimate is enough.

Calculating ROI

Add revenue attributable to SEO in the period, subtract all investment (fees, content, tools) and divide by the investment. Read the result with caution and over several months.

If it is still low, look at the trend in enquiries before deciding.

Reporting

A clear report with enquiries, attributed revenue, top pages and next actions. Avoid charts without interpretation.

Decision makers want to know if it works and what happens next.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Measuring only rankings.
  • Not recording client source.
  • Judging too early.
  • Ignoring part of the cost.
  • Reports with no conclusion.

How I explain it

Do not attribute everything to the penny. Measure enquiries, how many become sales, and what an average sale is worth. Three numbers give a sensible ROI.

And always compare with a baseline: where you were before. Without it, no ROI figure means anything.
