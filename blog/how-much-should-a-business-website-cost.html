What should
a business website
actually cost?

A realistic breakdown of website pricing, and how to judge whether a specific quote genuinely matches the work involved.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz• December 15, 2026• 7 min read

Website pricing spans an enormous range, from a few hundred euros for a template-based DIY site to tens of thousands for a fully custom build, and that range genuinely reflects real differences in underlying scope rather than businesses simply charging arbitrarily different amounts for genuinely comparable work. Understanding what actually drives the number makes a quote considerably easier to evaluate on its own merits, rather than judged purely against another quote's headline figure.

What genuinely drives the price up

Custom design work rather than a template, the number of unique page templates needed, custom functionality beyond standard content pages, integration complexity with existing business systems, and the amount of strategic planning and content work included alongside the build itself, all push cost up in ways that reflect real, additional time and expertise, not arbitrary margin tacked on for its own sake.

What a low quote often quietly doesn't include

A significantly below-market quote, worth treating with genuine caution rather than excitement or relief, frequently means a heavily templated build with minimal customisation to speak of, no real strategic input into structure or messaging, limited or no ongoing support after launch, and sometimes hidden costs that surface later, additional charges for what turns out to be considered a separate change request, or a platform lock-in that makes leaving expensive down the line.

A rough realistic range for common business types

A solid, professionally designed small business site with five to ten pages typically falls somewhere in the low thousands of euros, a range that assumes real strategic input rather than a purely templated build. A more complex site with custom functionality, an integrated booking system, membership features, extensive custom design work, usually runs from the mid to high thousands. Enterprise sites with extensive custom development and multiple integrated systems can run considerably higher still, into five figures or more, depending entirely on the genuine complexity involved.

Questions that reveal whether a quote genuinely fits the scope

What exactly is included in ongoing support after launch, and for precisely how long. Who owns the final files, code and content outright once the entire project completes. What happens, specifically and in writing, if the agreed scope needs to change partway through the project. Clear, specific answers to these questions matter considerably more for evaluating a quote than the headline number alone, since the same nominal price can represent very different actual value depending on the answers.

Why the cheapest option is rarely the cheapest choice

A cheap site that ends up needing a full rebuild within two years, or one that quietly costs real conversions month after month due to poor structure and unclear messaging, frequently ends up more expensive overall, once everything is tallied honestly, than a properly scoped project would have been from the very start, once the true lifetime cost gets accounted for rather than just the initial invoice. This is worth weighing honestly against upfront price alone when comparing website design quotes across different providers.

The real comparison worth making

Comparing quotes purely on the headline number, without asking what specifically is included, is the single most common mistake in evaluating website pricing. Two quotes with a meaningfully different number can represent nearly identical actual value once the full scope of each is properly understood side by side.
