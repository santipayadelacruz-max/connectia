How long does it take
to build a website?
Realistic timelines.

A website can be ready in three days or three months. Content and decisions set the pace far more than design does.

By Santiago Payá de la Cruz•October 23, 2026•8 min read

Timeline depends on scope

A one-page landing page can be ready in a day or two. A small multi-page site in about a week. A site with a shop, several languages or integrations can take weeks or months.

The right question is not how long a website takes, but how long yours takes, with your content and your approvals.

What slows projects down

Most delays do not come from design but from waiting: copy that does not arrive, missing images, postponed decisions or several people approving every change.

Scope changes mid-project and unplanned integrations also stretch timelines.

How a website in days is possible

It works with a tight scope, a clear brief, a structure agreed before design and one well-used review round. Bureaucracy is cut, not care.

Some work does not fit a short timeline: long copy from scratch, professional photography or complex integrations. They can come in a second phase.

Reference timelines by project type

As a guide, always depending on scope:

  • One-page landing page: 24 to 48 hours.
  • Site of 3 to 5 pages: 3 to 5 days.
  • Site of about 10 pages: one week.
  • Larger site with SEO structure: two weeks to a month.
  • Online shop: from a week if the catalogue is ready.

How to shorten your timeline

Prepare content before starting: copy, logo, photos and contact details. Name one decision maker who replies the same day. Ask for a focused first version and expand later.

Reserve time for review. One consolidated round is faster than many small ones.

What not to cut

Even on a short timeline, do not skip mobile speed, SEO structure or pre-launch testing. Those cuts are paid for later with a site that underperforms.

Measurement should launch with the site: without analytics you will not know what works.

Where it usually goes wrong

The most frequent errors:

  • Promising a timeline with no scope.
  • Starting without content.
  • Changing structure during design.
  • Not reserving review time.
  • Leaving SEO to a phase that never comes.

What really slows a project

Nearly always it is waiting for copy, photos and approvals. Design and build are the quick part; thinking through what you want to say is the slow part.

So my best tip: have copy, logo and photos ready before we start, and name one person who decides.
